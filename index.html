History

    The page is non-custodial; the escrow address is not. This page never holds, sees or moves crypto — it only records the agreement and each side's signed confirmations. Funds sit at the escrow address above, which is a single-key Bitcoin wallet, so whoever controls its key can move the funds. Nothing on this page can release them.

    What the signatures do and don't prove: each entry is signed by the key of the party who made it, and the first entry signs the whole deal body, so editing a term, an address or an entry breaks verification. They do not prove the deal is genuine: someone can always build a fresh deal from scratch with new keys. So compare the two fingerprints below out of band, confirm the escrow address on a call or a different app, send a small test amount first, and verify every transaction hash on a block explorer. A hash is typed in by hand, so treat it as a pointer — open it and check the amount and the destination yourself.
